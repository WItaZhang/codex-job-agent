# Cover letter outline

The structure follows university career-center guides for graduate and data science cover letters
([GWU Data Science Cover Letter Guide](https://dats.columbian.gwu.edu/student-resources/career-resources/cover-letter-guide.html),
[CMU Graduate Student Cover Letter Guide 2026](https://www.cmu.edu/career/documents/sample-resumes-cover-letters/graduate_student_cover_letter_guide_final_2026.pdf),
[UCR Grad Cover Letter Handout](https://careers.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2026-02/25-26_grad_cover_letter_handout.pdf)).
Their common guidance: one page; name the role and give a hook in the opening; 1–2 concrete examples with methods
and impact rather than the whole resume; the posting's own keywords; the applicant's own voice without
exaggeration.

```text
Dear <Name | Team> Hiring Team,

[P1 — why this role, one hook]
I am applying for the <exact title> role on <team>. <One sentence linking the team's core problem to the closest
thing I have done.> <Current status as the resume states it.>

[P2 — strongest evidence for the posting's first responsibility]
<Where/when> I built <what>. <How: method, data, tools.> <Result with the resume's exact number and scope.>

[P3 — a second angle the posting asks for]
<Second experience, a paper or a competition, same pattern, shorter.>

[P4 — fit and close]
<Why this team, citing the posting's own work, not generic praise.> Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
<Name>
```

| Paragraph | Question it answers | Common mistakes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Which role, and why me? | Only "I am excited to apply"; opening with the company's mission |
| P2 | Have I done the central work of this role? | Listing skills; paraphrasing the posting without evidence; numbers without scope |
| P3 | What else strengthens the case? | An unrelated topic; a team result presented as an individual one |
| P4 | Why this team? | Generic flattery; repeating P1; visas or salary |

- P2 may follow STAR without subheadings: one or two sentences of context, then actions and results.
- Use the posting's terms for its work; recruiters and screening systems look for them.
- A simple thank-you closes the letter; skip stock closings.
